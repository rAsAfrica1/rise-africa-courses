# INSTRUCTOR GUIDE
## Sustainable Pig Farming Course - Complete Implementation Guide

This guide shows you how to teach, manage, and grade the pig farming course.

---

## 📚 COURSE OVERVIEW (For Instructors)

### **What You're Teaching:**
A comprehensive 6-module, 19.5-hour online/farm-based pig farming course designed for African agricultural contexts.

### **How It Works:**
1. **Online learning** (videos, readings, quizzes) - ~8 hours
2. **Farm-based practice** (implementation steps) - ~9 hours  
3. **Project work** (business planning) - ~2.5 hours
4. **Instructor support** (grading, feedback) - your time

### **Key Philosophy:**
Students learn by DOING. The course is 50% watching videos and 50% farm work.

---

## 🎯 LEARNING OUTCOMES

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. ✅ Assess their region's suitability for pig farming
2. ✅ Select appropriate pig breeds for their climate
3. ✅ Design cost-effective, sustainable housing
4. ✅ Create balanced feeding programs using local resources
5. ✅ Implement disease prevention and health protocols
6. ✅ Manage breeding cycles and piglet care
7. ✅ Keep accurate farm records and financial tracking
8. ✅ Calculate farm profitability and ROI
9. ✅ Create a complete, implementable business plan
10. ✅ Start an actual pig farming operation

## 👥 STUDENT EXPECTATIONS

- Basic literacy (can read and write)
- Access to land/farm property
- Willing to do manual farm work
- Basic math ability (no advanced algebra needed)

### **Time Commitment:**
- **Online:** 8 hours over 6-12 weeks
- **Farm work:** 9 hours (spread across the course)
- **Projects:** 2.5 hours
- **Total:** ~20 hours over 6-13 weeks (flexible)

### **Success Indicators:**
- Completes all 6 modules
- Submits all projects on time
- Asks questions when stuck
- Engages with farm work (photos/documentation)
- Scores 70%+ on assessments

## 📖 HOW TO USE THE MODULES

### **Module Structure (All 6 are identical):**

Each module contains:
1. **Learning Objectives** (5-6 outcomes)
2. **5 Embedded Videos** (YouTube CC-licensed)
3. **Materials Checklist** (what they need)
4. **6 Implementation Steps** (farm activities)
5. **Success Criteria** (completion requirements)
6. **3-Question Quiz** (knowledge check)
7. **Real-World Project** (deliverable)
8. **Next Module Preview** (preview next topic)

### **Your Role Per Module:**

**Before Module Starts:**
- [ ] Review the module yourself
- [ ] Check all video links work
- [ ] Customize local information (vet contacts, prices)
- [ ] Prepare grading rubric
- [ ] Create submission deadline

**During Module (Student Learning):**
- [ ] Answer student questions (within 24 hours)
- [ ] Provide encouragement/motivation
- [ ] Check in on farm work (optional weekly check-ins)
- [ ] Monitor quiz submissions
- [ ] Help students stuck on implementation steps

**After Module (Grading):**
- [ ] Grade quiz (auto-graded, just verify)
- [ ] Grade project using rubric
- [ ] Provide feedback on project
- [ ] Document grades
- [ ] Send feedback within 3-5 days

## 🎓 GRADING RUBRIC

Use this rubric for EVERY project (same across all modules):

### **Module Project Grading (40% of module grade)**

**Completeness (20 points):**
- 20 pts: All required sections present, detailed
- 15 pts: Most sections present, some missing detail
- 10 pts: About half sections present
- 5 pts: Minimal sections, major gaps
- 0 pts: Missing or incomplete

**Realism (20 points):**
- 20 pts: Completely feasible in student's actual context
- 15 pts: Mostly feasible, minor adjustments needed
- 10 pts: Somewhat feasible, significant adjustments needed
- 5 pts: Questionable feasibility, many issues
- 0 pts: Unrealistic or not grounded in context

**Research (20 points):**
- 20 pts: Extensive research evident (farmer interviews, extension contacts, market data)
- 15 pts: Good research (3+ sources consulted)
- 10 pts: Some research (1-2 sources)
- 5 pts: Minimal research, mostly student's ideas
- 0 pts: No evidence of research

**Accuracy (20 points):**
- 20 pts: All calculations correct, facts accurate
- 15 pts: Most calculations correct (1-2 errors)
- 10 pts: Some errors but general approach sound
- 5 pts: Multiple errors affect conclusions
- 0 pts: Major errors or inaccurate information

**Clarity (20 points):**
- 20 pts: Well-written, easy to follow, professional
- 15 pts: Generally clear with minor issues
- 10 pts: Understandable but some confusing sections
- 5 pts: Difficult to follow, unclear writing
- 0 pts: Very difficult to understand

**Total Points: /100 (multiply by 0.4 for 40% weight)**

## ✏️ GRADING WORKFLOW

### **For Each Project Submission:**

**Step 1: Organize (5 min)**
- Download file
- Create grading folder
- Open rubric

**Step 2: Read (10 min)**
- Read entire project first
- Get sense of quality
- Note first impressions

**Step 3: Grade (15 min)**
- Score each rubric category (0-20 points)
- Total should be 80-100 for passing
- Below 70 = needs revision

**Step 4: Feedback (10 min)**
- Write 2-3 constructive comments
- Point out strengths
- Suggest improvements
- Email or comment in document

**Step 5: Record (2 min)**
- Document grade in your system
- Send feedback to student
- Archive the submission

**Total per project: ~40 minutes**

## 💬 PROVIDING FEEDBACK

### **Good Feedback (Students Want This):**

✅ **Specific:** "Your herd size of 20 pigs requires 16-20 m² of shelter space. Your design shows 12 m² - you'll need to expand."

✅ **Constructive:** "Good research on local pig breeds. Consider also checking market demand for breeding stock vs. meat pigs in your area."

✅ **Encouraging:** "Your financial projections show realistic costs. Break-even in month 8 is achievable if you maintain feed efficiency."

✅ **Actionable:** "Your project needs vet contact information. Call extension office or search 'veterinarian [your town]' and add that detail."

### **Feedback to Avoid:**

❌ **Vague:** "Needs more work" (more work on WHAT?)

❌ **Negative:** "This is wrong" (how to fix it?)

❌ **Discouraging:** "This won't work" (why not? how to improve?)

❌ **Off-topic:** Comments about grammar when assessing farm knowledge

### **Feedback Template:**

```
Dear [Student Name],

Thank you for submitting Module X project. Here's my feedback:

STRENGTHS:
- Well-researched market analysis
- Realistic budgeting approach
- Good farmer interviews documented

AREAS TO IMPROVE:
- Housing design needs ventilation details
- Feed costs seem high - compare with 2-3 more suppliers
- Breeding timeline could be earlier (month 4 vs. month 6)

NEXT STEPS:
- Revise housing design for ventilation
- Get 2 more feed supplier quotes
- Recalculate breeding timeline
- Resubmit revised version by [DATE]

Grade: 75/100 (Meets minimum - revision encouraged)

Let me know if you have questions!
[Your Name]
```

## 🆘 COMMON STUDENT ISSUES & SOLUTIONS

### **"I don't have land to do implementation steps"**

**Solution:**
- Use neighbor's farm for activities
- Partner with another student
- Work with agricultural extension office
- Document on borrowed land (still valid learning)
- Some steps can be research-based (interviews, market prices)

### **"The videos won't load"**

**Solution:**
- Check internet connection
- Try different browser
- Refresh page
- Videos need internet to load YouTube
- Provide transcript/summary if student has limited data

### **"I don't know what this means"**

**Solution:**
- Point them to the related video section
- Explain in simpler language
- Give an example from their context
- Connect them with extension office expert
- Never let them stay confused - clarify immediately

### **"I'm behind schedule"**

**Solution:**
- Reassure them - course is flexible
- Help them create catch-up plan
- Consider if they need extra support
- May need to extend deadline
- Check if farm work is realistic for their situation

### **"Can I skip this module?"**

**Solution:**
- NO - each builds on previous (except can skip intro)
- But if they have prior knowledge:
  - Still watch videos (local context matters)
  - Can do implementation steps quicker
  - Project can be abbreviated
  - Still complete assessment

### **"I don't understand the math"**

**Solution:**
- Walk them through calculation step-by-step
- Use concrete examples (10 pigs, 2 kg feed each)
- Provide calculation templates in Excel
- Connect with math tutor if needed
- Don't proceed until they understand

## 👨‍🏫 TEACHING BEST PRACTICES

### **DO:**
✅ Respond to student questions within 24 hours
✅ Encourage farm work (ask for photos)
✅ Celebrate progress (Module 1 complete = milestone!)
✅ Connect students with local experts (extension office)
✅ Be flexible on deadlines if students working on actual farms
✅ Share student success stories with other students
✅ Follow up if student goes quiet (check in)
✅ Provide sample answers/projects for reference

### **DON'T:**
❌ Just tell students answers (let them discover)
❌ Grade only on written work (farm evidence matters)
❌ Ignore late submissions (reach out first)
❌ Be harsh on grammar (assess knowledge, not English)
❌ Accept work that has no farm foundation
❌ Rush through feedback (it matters)
❌ Assume prior knowledge (explain everything)

## 🎓 FINAL CAPSTONE (Module 6)

This is the most important grade. It's a complete business plan.

### **Capstone Assessment:**

**What Student Submits:**
- 25-35 page comprehensive business plan
- All sections from Modules 1-6
- Professional formatting
- Photos/diagrams
- Ready to implement

**Grading Criteria:**
- Completeness: All 10+ sections present
- Realism: Feasible in student's actual context
- Research: Evidence of local farmer interviews, market research
- Accuracy: Calculations correct, projections realistic
- Clarity: Professional, well-written, easy to follow

**Scoring:**
- 90-100: Excellent - ready to bank for loan
- 80-89: Good - minor revisions needed
- 70-79: Acceptable - significant revisions needed
- Below 70: Needs major work - resubmit

**Use:** Student can actually use this to start their farm!
