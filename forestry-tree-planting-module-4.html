📊 Common Tree Diseases & Pests in Africa

Disease/Pest Affected Species Symptoms Prevention Treatment Cost
Leaf Spot (Fungal) Teak, Mahogany, Mango Brown/black spots on leaves, defoliation Prune infected branches, improve air flow, remove fallen leaves Free (cultural)
Root Rot (Phytophthora) All species if poorly drained Wilting, yellow leaves, tree death Ensure good drainage, avoid waterlogging, proper spacing Free (prevention)
Wood Borers Teak, Timber species Sawdust in bark, tunnel holes, weakened wood Regular monitoring, remove infested branches, promote tree vigor $2-5/tree spray if needed
Aphids & Scale Insects Mango, Acacia, many species Sticky residue, yellowing leaves, poor growth Strong water spray, encourage natural predators (ladybugs) Free-$2/tree spray
Canker (Fungal) Mahogany, Pine, Grafted trees Sunken lesions on trunk, bark splitting, branch dieback Prune 30 cm below lesion, sterilize tools, promote tree vigor $5-15/tree (pruning labor)

📋 6 Implementation Steps

Step 1: Establish Monitoring System

What to monitor: Monthly visual inspections of 10-20 random trees (all tree sections: leaves, branches, trunk, roots). Record observations: leaf color, spot patterns, visible insects, branch dieback, any abnormalities. Cost: $0 (time investment). Early detection saves 80% of trees.

Action: Create monitoring checklist, do first inspection within 1 week, establish monthly schedule.

Step 2: Implement Biosecurity Practices

Practices (FREE): Sterilize pruning tools (wipe with cloth after each cut), remove fallen leaves/branches (harbor diseases), avoid planting in poor-drainage areas, maintain tree spacing (prevents fungal spread), remove infected trees immediately. These simple steps prevent 60-70% of problems.

Action: Set up tool sterilization routine, create leaf removal schedule, assess drainage in plantation, plan spacing corrections.

Step 3: Promote Tree Vigor (Best Prevention)

Healthy trees resist disease: Proper nutrition, adequate water, good soil drainage, appropriate spacing - vigorous trees fight off pests/diseases naturally. This is the single most cost-effective strategy. Cost: already budgeted in soil/water/nutrition modules.

Action: Ensure all trees receive proper care per Modules 3-4 guidance. Prioritize vulnerable young trees.

Step 4: Use Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

IPM approach (avoids pesticides): Start with cultural control (pruning, spacing), then biological control (encourage beneficial insects like ladybugs), only use targeted spray as last resort. Pesticide cost: $2-5/tree when absolutely necessary, but 80% of problems solve without pesticides.

Action: Learn to identify beneficial insects (ladybugs, parasitic wasps). Encourage them by maintaining diverse vegetation around plantation.

Step 5: Respond Quickly to Detected Problems

When problem detected: (1) Confirm diagnosis (consult extension officer if uncertain), (2) Isolate if contagious, (3) Prune affected parts immediately (cut 30-50 cm below visible damage, burn/compost material), (4) Apply treatment if needed (organic spray $2-5, or chemical if severe). Speed is critical - 1-2 weeks delay can spread problem to 10x more trees.

Action: Have extension officer contact ready, plan for immediate access to basic tools (pruning saw), know safe disposal method for infected material.

Step 6: Maintain Treatment Records & Economics

Tracking: Document all problems, treatments, costs, outcomes. Example: 100 trees with leaf spot ($50 labor to prune) vs. 100 untreated (lose 30% yield = $300 loss). Record shows treatment ROI = 6:1. Year 2: prevention costs decrease as tree health improves.

Action: Create record sheet: date, problem type, trees affected, treatment cost, outcome. Calculate annual health budget: expect $500-2,000/year for 10-hectare plantation.

🚀 Real-World Project: Health Management Plan

Part 1: Species Health Profile

For your main planting species, research common diseases/pests: what are the main threats? What regions are most affected? Create 1-page summary with symptoms, seasonal patterns, treatment options.

Part 2: Monitoring System Design

Design monthly monitoring protocol: which trees to check (sample size), what to observe (leaves/branches/trunk), how to record findings, frequency of inspection. Create simple checklist form for field use.

Part 3: Biosecurity Planning

Create action plan for biosecurity: tool sterilization procedure, fallen leaf/branch removal schedule, spacing assessment, drainage improvements. Cost estimate for implementation (most practices are free).

Part 4: Pest Identification Guide

Create illustrated guide for 5 major pests/diseases in your region: symptoms, affected parts, lifecycle, damage potential, treatment options (cultural, biological, chemical). Photos or drawings if possible.

Part 5: Treatment Response Protocol

Write standard operating procedure: When problem detected → (1) confirm diagnosis, (2) assess severity, (3) isolate if needed, (4) determine treatment, (5) implement, (6) monitor results. Timeline for each step.

Part 6: Resource Inventory

Inventory available resources: pruning tools, storage for chemicals, access to extension officer, spraying equipment, compost for vigor. Identify gaps and plan to fill them ($50-500 budget typical).

Part 7: Budget for Health Management

5-year health budget: Year 1 (establishment, learning: $500-1,000), Year 2-5 (ongoing maintenance + prevention: $300-500/year). Calculate ROI: prevent 10-20% yield loss ($1,000-3,000 value) with $500 investment = 2-6:1 return.

Part 8: Emergency Response Plan

Create "What to do if major problem detected" flowchart: decision tree for different scenarios (widespread disease, major pest outbreak, tree death). Contact list (extension officer, suppliers, equipment). Storage for emergency supplies.
