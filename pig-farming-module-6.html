📋 Key Records to Keep

Essential Farm Records

  • ✓ Pig ID & Registry: ID number, breed, date of birth, weight at key stages
  • ✓ Feed Records: Date, amount, cost, type of feed given
  • ✓ Health Records: Vaccinations, medications, illnesses, vet visits, costs
  • ✓ Breeding Records: Heat dates, breeding dates, pregnancy, farrowing dates, litter size
  • ✓ Income Records: Date sold, pig weight, buyer, price received, total payment
  • ✓ Expense Records: All purchases (feed, supplies, vet, equipment), dates, amounts
  • ✓ Production Records: Daily observations, growth rates, feed conversion efficiency
  • ✓ Market Records: Market prices over time, buyer contacts, pricing trends

🔧 Implementation Steps

Week 6-8 Action Items

1
Set Up Record-Keeping System
  • Choose format: Notebook, Excel spreadsheet, or farm management app
  • Create templates for: pig ID, feed log, health log, breeding log, sales log
  • Set up backup system (keep copies or digital backups)
  • Decide on daily vs. weekly recording schedule
  • Test system - make sure it's actually usable
2
Calculate Actual Production Costs
  • List all expenses: feed, housing, health, labor, equipment
  • Identify fixed costs (shelter) vs. variable costs (feed)
  • Calculate cost per pig per week/month/growth cycle
  • Include hidden costs: water, electricity, veterinary, time
  • Compare with industry benchmarks for your region
3
Research Local Market Prices
  • Visit local markets and note pig prices (by weight, live vs. slaughtered)
  • Ask farmers what they get paid for finished pigs
  • Track prices over several months to understand seasonality
  • Identify buyers (butchers, restaurants, traders, neighbors)
  • Calculate realistic revenue per pig for your area
4
Create Profitability Scenarios
  • Best Case: High prices, low mortality, efficient feed use
  • Expected Case: Average prices, normal mortality (10%), typical performance
  • Worst Case: Low prices, disease outbreak, poor growth
  • Calculate profit for each scenario
  • Identify break-even point and payback period
5
Build 12-Month Financial Projection
  • Create monthly revenue and expense projections
  • Account for seasonal price variations and production cycles
  • Include startup costs amortized over time
  • Show cumulative profit/loss by month
  • Identify when you reach break-even and positive cash flow
6
Create Performance Metrics Dashboard
  • Feed conversion ratio (kg feed : kg pig gain)
  • Days to market weight (faster = better)
  • Mortality rate (% deaths)
  • Cost per kg gain
  • Revenue per pig
  • Profit margin (%)
  • Track these monthly and compare to targets

📝 Knowledge Check Quiz

Question 1: What is "break-even point"?

Correct: B - Break-even is when you recover all costs but haven't made profit yet.

Question 2: Which should be kept track of for profitability analysis?

Correct: B - Small costs add up. Complete records show where money goes and where to save.

Question 3: What does "feed conversion ratio" measure?

Correct: B - FCR is KEY - lower FCR = more profit (less feed wasted).
