🍽️ Pig Nutrition Requirements

Typical Daily Feed Consumption & Costs

Life Stage Weight (kg) Daily Feed (kg) Protein Need (%) Est. Cost/Month
Piglets (weaned) 5-10 0.3-0.5 18-20% $8-15
Growers 25-60 1.5-2.0 15-18% $20-35
Finishers 60-110 2.0-2.5 12-16% $25-40
Breeding Sows 150-200 2.0-3.0 13-16% $30-50

Common Local Feed Ingredients & Their Value

Energy Sources

  • Grains: Maize, sorghum, millet (60-75% of diet) - Good energy source, relatively affordable
  • Root crops: Cassava, sweet potato (30-40% of diet) - Lower protein, good energy, often waste products available
  • Legumes: Beans, peas, groundnuts (protein + energy) - 15-25% protein, helps balance diet

Protein Sources

  • Legumes: Soybeans, groundnut cake (35-50% protein) - Premium but essential for young pigs
  • Fishmeal or insect protein: (60-70% protein) - Excellent if available, may be expensive
  • Food scraps: Vegetable waste, brewery waste, restaurant refuse - Free protein source if cleaned properly
  • Green forage: Alfalfa, improved pasture (10-15% protein) - Fresh or dried, excellent supplement

🔧 Implementation Steps

Week 3-4 Action Items

1
Research Local Feed Availability & Pricing
  • Identify 5-10 local feed sources: grain mills, legume suppliers, vegetable markets, food processors
  • Get current pricing for: maize, cassava, soybeans, legumes, commercial feed
  • Ask about seasonal price variations and availability
  • Identify free/waste feed sources: brewery waste, market scraps, kitchen refuse
  • Create price comparison table
2
Calculate Feed Requirements for Your Herd
  • Decide your stocking plan: e.g., 2 breeding sows + 20 grower pigs
  • Use table above to calculate total daily feed needed (kg)
  • Calculate monthly feed needs: daily feed × 30 days
  • Document in spreadsheet with production cycle timeline
  • Example: 20 grower pigs × 1.8 kg/day = 36 kg/day = 1,080 kg/month
3
Create 3 Alternative Feeding Programs
  • Program A: 100% commercial feed (most expensive, fastest growth)
  • Program B: Mix of local grains + protein (moderate cost and growth)
  • Program C: Mostly local feed + waste products (cheapest, slower growth)
  • Calculate monthly feed cost for each program
  • Estimate growth rates and profitability for each option
4
Develop Your Feed Ration Recipes
  • Choose one feeding program from step 3
  • For each life stage (piglets, growers, finishers), create a recipe
  • Example grower pig ration: 60% maize + 20% cassava + 15% legumes + 5% supplement = 100%
  • Verify protein content meets requirements (see table above)
  • Test recipe with experienced farmers - ask if they've used similar combinations
5
Set Up Feeding System & Schedules
  • Decide on feeding frequency: Once daily (morning) vs twice daily (morning + evening)
  • Feeding pigs by weight group: separate piglets, growers, finishers for right nutrition
  • Plan feeding infrastructure: troughs, feeders, water systems
  • Create daily feeding schedule and portion sizes
  • Plan feed storage: keep dry, protected from pests and rain
6
Create 12-Month Feed Budget & Supplier Contracts
  • Project monthly feed costs for your entire herd over 12 months
  • Account for seasonal price changes and production cycles
  • Identify suppliers and negotiate pricing/agreements
  • Create backup plans for feed shortage or price spikes
  • Plan for contingencies: drought, crop failure, market disruptions

📝 Knowledge Check Quiz

Question 1: What is the typical daily feed requirement for a 50 kg grower pig?

Correct Answer: B - Grower pigs need 1.5-2.0 kg daily, which is about 3% of body weight.

Question 2: Which ingredient should NOT be more than 40% of a grower pig's diet?

Correct Answer: B - Cassava is low in protein and can cause digestive upset if over-used.

Question 3: What is the primary advantage of using local feed sources?

Correct Answer: A - Local feeds reduce costs and support circular farming systems.
