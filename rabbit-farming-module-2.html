📋 6 Implementation Steps

Step 1: Choose Housing System by Scale

Hobby (2-3 rabbits): single wooden hutches ($50-100 each). Small farm (5-10): stacked cage system ($200-300). Serious production (20+): colony system or commercial shed. Key consideration: daily management time. Single cage = 15 min/day for 3 rabbits. Colony = 20 min/day for 15 rabbits (4x more efficient). Space: 4-6 rabbits per m² in commercial systems.

Step 2: Build or Acquire Cages

DIY: wood frame + 1/2" wire mesh + plywood bottom = $15-25 per cage. Commercial: quality cages $30-50 each. Cage size: standard 60cm L × 45cm W × 35cm H (suitable for New Zealand White). Space: does = slightly larger than bucks (breeding females need more comfort). Essential: smooth floors (prevents sore hocks), proper ventilation, easy cleaning access.

Step 3: Install Feeding & Watering Systems

Water: hanging bottles ($5 each, change daily) or automatic water lines (more expensive, saves labor). Feeders: manual bowls (cleaned 2x daily) or automatic feeders ($20-50, requires less daily work). Cost-benefit: spend $100 on automation to save 30 min daily labor (worth it above 20 rabbits).

Step 4: Control Temperature & Light

Optimal: 15-20°C, 40-60% humidity. Avoid: direct sunlight (heat stress at >25°C), drafts, damp conditions. In tropics: shade (tarps), ventilation (fans $30-50), possibly evaporative cooling ($100+). In cold climates: insulation, possible supplemental heating ($50-200). Light: 10-14 hrs/day optimal for breeding/production. Budget $50-200 for climate control depending on region.

Step 5: Manage Waste & Sanitation

Rabbits produce 10-15kg waste per rabbit per month (excellent fertilizer). Slotted floors allow waste to fall through to collection area below (easier cleaning). Alternative: deep litter system (change monthly, composting in place). Daily: remove soiled hay, top up feed. Weekly: full cage cleaning (essential for disease prevention). Sanitation saves veterinary costs 10x over.

Step 6: Plan for Scalability

Start small (3-5 does): prove model, learn management, test market. Expand: add 1-2 does every 2-3 months as cash flow allows. At 10 does: consider shelter upgrade (dedicated rabbit shed). Labor: 3-5 does need 20-30 min/day. 20 does = 60-80 min/day. Plan hiring (part-time help at 15+ rabbits) or rationalize expansion to match available time.

🚀 Real-World Project: Housing System Design & Build

Part 1: System Selection & Planning

Based on your target herd size, choose housing system. Sketch design: number of cages, arrangement (vertical stacking? rows?), ventilation, water/feed system. Calculate: total cost ($___), space needed (m²), labor time/day (min).

Part 2: Material Sourcing & Budget

Get quotes: wire mesh, wood, feeders, waterers, tools. Total material cost: ___. DIY vs. buy decision: time to build (hrs?) vs. cost to purchase commercial. Amortized over 5 years, what's cheapest?

Part 3: Construction & Setup

Build/acquire cages. Install water system (bottles or lines + backups). Set up feeding station. Create waste collection area. Test: can you access every rabbit for handling? Water flowing properly? Adequate ventilation?

Part 4: Climate Control Planning

Assess your location: typical temperatures year-round. Design: cooling strategy (shade/fans/water) for summer, insulation/heating for winter if needed. Cost estimate: ___. Implementation: prioritize based on climate extremes in your area.

Part 5: Daily Routine Documentation

Create checklist: morning tasks (check water, top-up feed), evening (check all rabbits for health), weekly (deep clean cages), monthly (facility maintenance). Time study: track actual hours for 1 week. Identify time-savers for next iteration.

Part 6: Sanitation Protocol

Define: daily cleaning (remove uneaten food, spot-clean), weekly deep clean (entire cage, disinfect), monthly deep disinfection (cages removed, spray). Cost: cleaning supplies (___). Track: disease outbreaks (target: zero in first 6 months = proof of good sanitation).

Part 7: Safety & Welfare Assessment

Checklist: no sharp edges (sore feet), proper cage size, adequate ventilation, temperature within range, water always fresh. Observe rabbits: healthy posture? good appetite? normal behavior? Adjust if any concerns.

Part 8: Scalability Plan

Current: 3-5 rabbits, current system. Plan: at what point upgrade housing? (after 10 rabbits? when expanding to 20?). Space requirements, labor requirements, cost. Timeline: realistic expansion pace based on profits & available time/money.
