🦠 Common Pig Diseases in Africa

Quick Reference: Major Diseases

African Swine Fever (ASF)

Very serious, highly contagious viral disease. Symptoms: fever, weakness, loss of appetite, death. PREVENTION: Strict biosecurity, no wild pigs, proper waste disposal.

Foot-and-Mouth Disease

Viral - blisters on hooves and mouth. Pigs recover but lose weight. PREVENTION: Vaccination, quarantine infected animals, safe feed storage.

Swine Fever (Hog Cholera)

Viral disease causing fever, diarrhea, weakness. Needs vaccination. PREVENTION: Vaccination program essential in endemic areas.

Parasites (Internal & External)

Roundworms, lice, mange cause poor growth and anemia. PREVENTION: Regular deworming (every 6-8 weeks), clean housing, wallows for external parasites.

Respiratory Disease (Pneumonia)

Bacterial - coughing, difficulty breathing, poor growth. PREVENTION: Good ventilation, avoid dust and ammonia, manage humidity.

Diarrhea (Scours)

Often in young pigs, caused by E.coli or poor feeding. PREVENTION: Clean water, proper hygiene, good nutrition, sanitation.

🛡️ Disease Prevention Checklist

Essential Prevention Practices

  • ✓ Biosecurity: Restrict visitor access, use dedicated shoes/clothing for pig area
  • ✓ Hygiene: Clean housing regularly (weekly minimum), remove manure and urine daily
  • ✓ Water: Provide clean, fresh water 24/7 - never from stagnant sources
  • ✓ Feed: Store safely to prevent contamination and pest access
  • ✓ Isolation: Separate new pigs for 2-3 weeks before mixing with herd
  • ✓ Vaccination: Follow local veterinary vaccination recommendations
  • ✓ Monitoring: Check pigs daily for signs of illness
  • ✓ Records: Keep detailed health and medication records
  • ✓ Veterinary Care: Build relationship with local veterinarian
  • ✓ Nutrition: Proper feeding prevents nutritional deficiency diseases

🔧 Implementation Steps

Week 4-5 Action Items

1
Research Local Veterinary Services
  • Find nearby veterinarians or veterinary clinics
  • Get their contact info, hours, emergency services, pricing
  • Ask about vaccination programs and disease monitoring
  • Find agricultural extension officers who can advise on livestock health
  • Build relationships before you have a problem
2
Create a Vaccination & Deworming Plan
  • Consult with veterinarian about vaccines needed in your region
  • Plan vaccination schedule for piglets, growers, breeding stock
  • Schedule deworming: typically every 6-8 weeks for young pigs, 2-3x yearly for adults
  • Document dates and products used
  • Calculate annual health costs
3
Establish Biosecurity Protocols
  • Design visitor control procedures (limited access to pig area)
  • Create isolation area for new pigs (separate pen, 2-3 week quarantine)
  • Establish cleaning schedule and procedures for housing
  • Plan waste management (manure pile location, proper disposal)
  • Identify risks on your property (wild pigs, rodents, contaminated water sources)
4
Set Up Health Monitoring System
  • Create daily observation checklist for each pig
  • Document: appetite, activity level, coat condition, fecal appearance, respiratory signs
  • Keep health records for each pig (ID, weight, treatments, vaccines)
  • Plan early detection: know signs of common diseases
  • Establish action plan: when to call veterinarian
5
Create Emergency Response Plan
  • Document veterinary contact (phone, address, hours)
  • Plan for disease outbreak: isolation area, sick pig care, treatment options
  • Know animal sacrifice procedures if needed (euthanasia requirements in your area)
  • Plan for feed/water shortage
  • Have emergency funds budgeted for health crises
6
Create Your Health & Veterinary Budget
  • Calculate annual vaccination costs
  • Estimate deworming expenses
  • Budget for emergency veterinary care
  • Factor in preventive medications if needed
  • Total annual health budget per pig
