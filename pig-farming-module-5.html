♻️ Pig Reproductive Timeline

Key Reproductive Milestones

Stage Age/Timing Key Details
Puberty 16-24 weeks (4-6 months) First estrous cycle appears. Not recommended to breed at first cycle - wait for maturity.
First Breeding 24-32 weeks (6-8 months) Gilts (young females) ready if weight is 120-150 kg. Must be well-grown and healthy.
Estrous Cycle 21 days average Heat lasts 2-3 days. Sows cycle year-round in proper conditions.
Gestation 114-116 days (~3.8 months) From breeding to farrowing. Sow pregnant with typically 8-12 piglets.
Farrowing 114-116 days after breeding Giving birth, typically 2-4 hours for whole litter. Usually 8-12 piglets born.
Lactation 6-8 weeks Piglets nurse from mother. Weaning at 3-4 weeks is common (piglets then need starter feed).
Reproductive Life 4-6 years Quality sows can produce 5-6+ litters per lifetime (2 litters per year typical).

🔥 Signs a Sow is in Heat

How to Recognize Breeding Time

  • ✓ Swelling of vulva - Pink, puffy, may have mucus discharge
  • ✓ Restlessness - Pacing, trying to escape pen, mounting other pigs
  • ✓ Standing reflex - Stands still when boar/person applies pressure to back (key sign)
  • ✓ Vocalization - Grunting and vocalizing more than usual
  • ✓ Loss of appetite - May not eat normally for a day or two
  • ✓ Best breeding window - Heat lasts 2-3 days; breed on day 1-2 of heat

🔧 Implementation Steps

Week 5-6 Action Items

1
Select & Obtain Breeding Stock
  • Source quality breeding sow (6-8 months old, healthy, good genetics)
  • Arrange boar access: on-farm boar, hire for breeding, or artificial insemination
  • Verify health status of breeding animals (vaccinations, disease-free)
  • Document breeding animal records (age, breed, health, performance)
  • Plan backup breeding options in case of infertility
2
Monitor Heat Cycles & Record Dates
  • Start recording sow observations daily (beginning 2 weeks after purchase)
  • Document: date of heat signs, duration, breeding date if bred
  • Create simple spreadsheet tracking: name, date of heat, date bred, expected due date
  • Test standing reflex daily during suspected heat
  • If using boar, confirm successful mating occurred
3
Prepare Farrowing Pen/Nest
  • Build/repair farrowing pen: safe, draft-free, temperature-controlled (22-25°C ideal)
  • Provide nesting materials: straw or hay for bedding
  • Install heat lamp for piglets (if farrowing in cool season)
  • Ensure good drainage and ventilation
  • Keep pen clean and disinfected before sow enters
4
Provide Proper Pregnancy Care
  • Feed pregnant sow high-quality diet with proper nutrition (increase feed 2nd-3rd trimester)
  • Provide clean water 24/7
  • Monitor health: watch for signs of problems (lethargy, lack of appetite, discharge)
  • Exercise: allow movement (farrowing sow shouldn't be in small pen entire pregnancy)
  • Health checks: maintain vaccination schedule and parasite control
5
Prepare for Farrowing Event
  • Signs labor is near: swollen vulva, leaking milk, restlessness 12-24 hours before
  • Prepare supplies: clean towels, disinfectant, iodine for piglet navels, heat source
  • Know when to call veterinarian: straining >2 hours with no piglet, piglet stuck, sow collapse
  • Plan monitoring: observe farrowing process but don't interfere unless necessary
  • Have emergency cash for veterinary help if needed
6
Create Newborn Piglet Care Plan
  • Colostrum (first milk): ensure all piglets nurse within first 6 hours
  • Disinfect umbilical cords with iodine to prevent infection
  • Piglet mortality prevention: monitor for crushing, provide heat lamp
  • Early nutrition: introduce starter feed at 2-3 weeks (before weaning at 4-6 weeks)
  • Health care: vitamin injections if needed, tail docking, castration procedures (region-dependent)

📝 Knowledge Check Quiz

Correct: B - Pigs gestate ~114-116 days, about 3.8 months.

Question 2: What is the "standing reflex"?

Correct: B - The standing reflex is the KEY sign a sow is in heat and ready to breed.

Question 3: How many piglets typically born in one litter?

Correct: B - A healthy sow typically produces 8-12 piglets per litter.
