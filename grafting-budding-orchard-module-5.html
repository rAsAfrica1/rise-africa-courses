📋 6 Implementation Steps

Step 1: Select Grafting Method

Choose: cleft (high success, longer cycle), budding (faster, consistent quality), side graft (medium complexity), whip graft (premium results). Match to your resources: time, budget, expertise level. Budget: $0.30-2.00 per graft for materials & labor.

Step 2: Prepare Rootstocks

Source quality rootstocks: nursery-grown or raise your own. Species: mango (local/hybrid), citrus (compatible types), avocado, macadamia. Prepare: lift dormancy if needed, harden off, graft at right growth stage. Timing critical: success depends on growth vigor.

Step 3: Select & Collect Scion Wood

Choose healthy, disease-free donor trees with desired traits (yield, size, taste, disease resistance). Collect: correct growth stage (semi-hardwood, not too tender). Storage: keep cool, moist until grafting (24-48 hours max). Quality scion = higher success rates.

Step 4: Perform Grafting/Budding

Technique: clean cuts, proper alignment, secure binding (tape/raffia). Sterilize tools (alcohol, 10% bleach). Work cleanly to prevent contamination. After graft: protect from water/direct sun for 2-3 weeks. Monitor: remove dead buds, watch for healing signs.

Step 5: Care During Establishment

Water: keep rootstock moist but not waterlogged. Nutrients: balanced fertilizer monthly. Prune: remove competing shoots below graft, support scion if needed. Timeline: budding shows leaves in 1-3 weeks, full establishment 3-6 months. Success indicator: scion growth.

Step 6: Grow Out & Market

Nursery: grow grafted trees 1-2 years before sale (size/quality improves value). Field: plant out & establish (3-5 years to commercial production). Revenue: grafted trees $5-20 each (premium over $0.50-1.00 cost). Profitability: at 80% success, 1,000 grafts = $8,000-16,000 revenue minus $500-2,000 cost = 75%+ margin.

🚀 Real-World Project: Grafting Operation

Part 1: Method Selection

Research grafting methods. Assess your resources. Choose best-fit method. Plan: materials, tools, labor timeline.

Part 2: Rootstock & Scion Sourcing

Identify suppliers. Negotiate prices. Calculate: cost per graft, total budget for 500-1,000 grafts. Timeline: when can you start?

Part 3: Trial Run

Start small: 50-100 grafts. Track: success rate %, cost per successful tree, timeline to saleable size. Learn from failures. Refine technique.

Part 4: Scale Plan

Based on trial results, scale to 500+ grafts/season. Equipment: workspace, tools, materials storage. Labor: hours/day needed, cost per graft.

Part 5: Market Research

Identify buyers: farmers wanting improved varieties, nurseries, agricultural projects. Price research: what do buyers pay for grafted trees? Demand estimate: market size (_____ trees/year).

Part 6: Financial Model

Cost per tree: materials + labor. Revenue per tree: $___. Success rate: __%. Profitability: at 80% success, profit per tree = $___. Annual target: _____ trees × profit = $_____ income.

Part 7: Quality Standards

Define: tree size, graft health, root development standards at sale. Implement quality checks before selling. Build reputation for quality = premium prices & repeat customers.

Part 8: Growth Plan

Year 1: prove viability with trial. Year 2: scale to 500 grafts. Year 3: 2,000 grafts/season. Facility needs? Labor needs? Market capacity? Timeline to $10k+ annual income from grafting?
