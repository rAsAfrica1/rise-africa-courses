📋 6 Implementation Steps

Step 1: Choose Your Substrate & Gather Materials

Oyster mushrooms: straw ($0.05-0.10/kg) + coffee grounds (free). Shiitake: hardwood sawdust ($0.15-0.30/kg). Button: compost ($0.20-0.40/kg). For 100 kg substrate blocks: 100kg base material ($5-30 cost), water, spawn 5-10% by weight (5-10kg spawn at $2-5/kg = $10-50).

Step 2: Prepare Substrate Mix

Recipe example: 70% straw, 25% sawdust, 5% lime (balances pH). Mix in large tub or pile, breaking up clumps. Wet mixture to 65-75% moisture (squeeze test: few drops between fingers). Aim for uniform, lump-free mix. Bad mixing = uneven colonization = lower yield.

Step 3: Sterilize Substrate (Kill Competitors)

Option A - Boiling: Fill large pot, bring to rolling boil 1-2 hours. Option B - Steaming: 1-1.5 hours over boiling water. Option C - Pressure cooker (bags): 15 PSI × 2.5 hours (most effective). Cost: minimal (firewood/electricity $1-2). Sterilization = prevent contaminant fungi/bacteria competing with mushroom mycelium.

Step 4: Cool & Pack Substrate

After sterilization, let cool to 25-30°C (touchable but still warm). Pack into bags (1-2 kg per bag for oyster) or containers while still warm. Zip bags but leave small opening for air. Work in cleanest area possible. Rushed/dirty packing = high contamination rates.

Step 5: Inoculate with Quality Spawn

Spawn = mycelium growing on grain/sawdust. Mix spawn into bags (5-10% by weight): for 1kg substrate, add 50-100g spawn. Use sterile technique: clean hands/tools with alcohol. Break up spawn gently when mixing. Good spawn = white, clean smell, no green/black mold. Bad spawn = wasted substrate + empty results.

Step 6: Incubate for Colonization

Store bags at 20-25°C (room temperature), dark area. Oyster: colonization 7-14 days (bags turn white). Shiitake: 30-60 days. During colonization: no watering needed, minimal monitoring. After colonization complete = ready to fruit. Timeline: Week 1-2 (colonization), Week 3+ (fruiting begins) = mushrooms ready to harvest.

🚀 Real-World Project: Substrate Preparation Plan

Part 1: Substrate Material Sourcing

Identify suppliers in your area: straw (farmer), sawdust (lumber mill, furniture maker), coffee grounds (local cafe), compost (composting center). Negotiate pricing and delivery. Plan: 100kg substrate blocks need ___ kg materials, total cost ___.

Part 2: Recipe Development & Testing

Mix test batches (5-10 kg): Recipe 1 (70% straw + 30% sawdust), Recipe 2 (80% straw + 20% coffee + 5% lime). Document: materials, costs, mixing time, inoculation results, colonization speed, yield. Identify best recipe for your mushroom type & budget.

Part 3: Sterilization Method Setup

Select method based on available equipment: boiling (large pot), steaming (pressure cooker), oven (if temperature-controlled). Calculate: energy cost per batch, time per batch, capacity (kg/day sterilized). Test with 10kg batch, record colonization rates.

Part 4: Contamination Prevention Protocol

Document: workspace cleaning procedure, hand hygiene (before inoculation), tool disinfection (alcohol wipes), work area isolation. Track: contamination rate % on test batches. Goal: <10% contamination rate (>90% success).

Part 5: Spawn Quality Assessment

Find reliable spawn source: supplier recommendation (cost/kg), quality indicators (white color, clean smell, no mold), minimum order (kg), delivery time. Budget spawn costs: for 100kg substrate need 5-10kg spawn = ___. Test spawn quality on 5 bags.

Part 6: Colonization Monitoring System

Create tracking chart: bag ID, substrate type, spawn supplier, inoculation date, colonization start date, 100% colonization date, contamination observed. Average colonization time: ___. Success rate: ___%. Adjust procedures if <90% success.

Part 7: Cost Analysis per Block

Track costs: substrate materials ($), spawn ($), sterilization energy ($), labor (hours × $/hr), packaging ($). Total cost per 1kg block: ___. Expected yield: 3-4 flushes × 0.3kg mushrooms/flush = 0.9-1.2kg fresh mushrooms. Revenue: 1kg × $5-10/kg = $5-10. Profit per block: ___.

Part 8: Scaling Production Plan

Weekly production goal: ___ blocks/week. Monthly: ___ blocks/month. Equipment needed: sterilization capacity (kg/day), drying space, storage (temperature, humidity). Labor: hours/day needed. Plan: when can you move to 500+ blocks/month production?
