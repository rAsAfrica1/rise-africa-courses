🔧 Implementation Steps

Complete these 6 steps this week. Each step builds on the previous one.

Week 1 Action Items

1
Research Poultry Farming in Your Region
  • Contact local agricultural extension office
  • Ask about climate suitability for poultry
  • Interview 3 existing poultry farmers
  • Identify: breeds kept locally, challenges faced, profitability
  • Document findings in notes
2
Assess Your Available Space & Resources
  • Measure available space on your property
  • Note: sunlight exposure, water access, predator risks
  • Take photos from multiple angles
  • Identify best location for chicken house/run
  • Assess existing structures (can they be modified?)
3
Calculate Startup Costs & Initial Budget
  • Research local prices: chicks, feeders, waterers, housing materials
  • Create spreadsheet with: startup costs (housing, equipment), ongoing costs (feed, vet)
  • Calculate: How many birds can you start with? What's break-even?
  • Note: Poultry ROI is faster than livestock (3-4 months)
  • Compare with pig or goat farming costs
4
Identify Your Target Poultry Species & Breeds
  • Research 3-4 poultry options: layer chickens, broiler chickens, ducks, guinea fowl
  • Compare: egg production, meat yield, feed efficiency, market demand, disease resistance
  • Best for Africa: Rhode Island Red, Leghorn (layers), Broiler crosses (meat)
  • Find local sources: Where can you buy chicks or birds locally?
  • Make top choice with detailed reasoning
5
Research Feed & Nutrition Available Locally
  • Learn poultry nutritional needs (protein, grains, greens, minerals)
  • Identify local feed options: grains, kitchen scraps, commercial feed, green forage
  • Calculate: Feed cost per bird per month
  • Explore: Can you produce any feed on your farm? (grain, vegetables, insects)
  • Contact 2-3 feed suppliers - compare prices
6
Create Your 12-Month Poultry Business Plan
  • Document all findings from steps 1-5
  • Create timeline: Month 1 (prep), Month 2 (build housing), Month 3 (buy birds), Months 4-12 (operations)
  • List: resource needs, budget requirements, risk mitigation
  • Share plan with experienced poultry farmer for feedback
  • Be ready to discuss in project submission

Correct Answer: B - Poultry's rapid growth cycle and low input needs make it ideal for African smallholders.

Question 2: Which poultry type is BEST for consistent egg income?
