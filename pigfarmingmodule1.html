🔧 Implementation Steps

Complete these 6 steps this week. Each step builds on the previous one.

Week 1 Action Items

1
Research Pig Farming in Your Region
Gather information about local pig farming practices and climate suitability
What to do:
  • Contact your local agricultural extension office
  • Ask about: average rainfall, temperature range, and rainy seasons in your area
  • Ask 3 existing local pig farmers about their experience and challenges
  • Document: climate data and 3 farmer interviews
  • Take notes on what works well in your specific region
2
Assess Your Available Land & Resources
Evaluate your current property and what you can dedicate to pig farming
What to do:
  • Walk your property and measure available space (in square meters)
  • Note: Water source location, drainage, distance to neighbors
  • Take photos from multiple angles
  • Identify: best location for pigsty (shelter), feeding area, waste management
  • Note: Any existing structures that can be modified
3
Create Your Initial Budget & Cost Estimate
Calculate startup costs for a small pig farm operation
What to do:
  • Research local prices for: young pigs, feeds, building materials, fencing
  • Create a spreadsheet with: startup costs (land prep, shelter), ongoing costs (feed, vet care), revenue potential
  • Calculate: How many pigs can you start with? What's your break-even point?
  • Note: Consider seasonal price variations
4
Identify Your Target Pig Breed(s)
Choose which breeds are best for your situation
What to do:
  • Research 3-4 pig breeds suitable for African conditions
  • Compare: Growth rate, feed efficiency, climate tolerance, market demand
  • Best breeds for Africa: Large Black, Kunekune, crosses with local genetics
  • Find local sources: Where can you buy breeding pigs locally?
  • Document your top choice with reasons
5
Learn About Feed & Nutrition Requirements
Understand what pigs eat and how to source affordable feed
What to do:
  • Research pig nutritional needs (protein, vitamins, minerals)
  • Identify: Local feed options (grains, kitchen scraps, crop residues, commercial feed)
  • Calculate: Feed costs per pig per month
  • Explore: Can you produce any feed on your farm? (fodder, vegetable scraps)
  • Connect with: 2-3 local feed suppliers to compare prices
6
Create Your 12-Month Pig Farming Plan
Write a comprehensive plan for your first year
What to do:
  • Document all findings from steps 1-5
  • Create a timeline: Month 1 (prep), Month 2-3 (build shelter), Month 4 (buy pigs), Months 5-12 (operations)
  • List: Resource needs, budget requirements, risk mitigation strategies
  • Share your plan with an experienced farmer for feedback
  • Be ready to discuss your plan in the project submission

🎯 Real-World Project

Project: Develop Your Pig Farming Business Plan

Take everything you've learned in this module and create a comprehensive 12-month business plan for starting your pig farming operation. This should be a real, detailed plan that you can use to guide your actual farming venture.

Required Deliverables:

  • Executive Summary (1 page): Overview of your pig farming idea, location, and goals
  • Climate & Location Assessment (1 page): Your region's suitability, weather patterns, local farming context
  • Market Analysis (1 page): Local demand for pork, prices, competition, your target market
  • Pig Breed Selection (1 page): Your chosen breed(s) and why they're right for your situation
  • Land & Facility Plan (1-2 pages): Your property layout, shelter design, feeding areas, waste management
  • Budget & Financial Projections (1-2 pages): Startup costs, monthly operational costs, revenue projections for 12 months
  • Feed Strategy (1 page): Feed sources, nutrition plan, cost estimates, local supplier information
  • Risk Management Plan (1 page): Potential problems you might face and how you'll prevent/solve them
  • 12-Month Implementation Timeline: Specific milestones and dates
  • References & Resources: Links to 5+ credible farming resources you used
  • Farmer Interviews Summary: Notes from your 3+ interviews with experienced farmers
📌 Submission Format: PDF document (10-15 pages), plus photos of your property. Email to your instructor or upload to the platform.
⭐ Grading Criteria:
  • Completeness: All sections included
  • Realism: Plan is feasible in your specific context
  • Research: Evidence of thorough research and farmer interviews
  • Financial accuracy: Budget calculations are realistic
  • Clarity: Well-written, easy to follow
