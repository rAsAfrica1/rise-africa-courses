📋 6 Implementation Steps

Step 1: Identify Optimal Harvest Timing

Oyster mushrooms: harvest when caps start curling slightly upward (button stage, 2-4cm cap). Timing: typically 7-10 days after pinning. Earlier (smaller) = premium price but fewer mushrooms. Later (larger) = more weight but lower price. Find the sweet spot: button stage 70% of harvest, mature stage 30%.

Step 2: Harvest with Proper Technique

Method: Grasp cluster at base, twist/pull gently from substrate. Use clean knife to cut if needed. Do NOT yank (damages bag, reduces flushes). Timing: harvest in morning when caps are firm, not soft/waterlogged. Handle gently - bruised mushrooms = lower grade = lower price ($3-4/kg vs. $8-10/kg).

Step 3: Cool & Dry Immediately After Harvest

Mushrooms = 90%+ water. Cooling slows respiration. In field/farm: place in cool area within 30 minutes of harvest. Ideal: 5-10°C, <90% humidity (paradoxically). Cost: small refrigerator ($200-400) for serious production. Dry slightly: allow air circulation for 2-4 hours to remove surface moisture (helps with storage).

Step 4: Sort & Grade by Quality

Grade A (Premium): button stage, 3-4cm cap, no bruises = $8-10/kg. Grade B (Good): mature stage, 4-6cm cap, minor imperfections = $5-7/kg. Grade C (Processor): misshapen, oversize, processing-only = $2-4/kg. Sorting time: ~5 minutes per kg. Impact: 50% Grade A + 40% Grade B + 10% Grade C = blended price $6.80/kg (vs. $4/kg if unsorted).

Step 5: Package for Market

Retail: 250g or 500g clamshell containers ($0.20-0.50 each). Include label with farm name, date, care instructions. Wholesale: bulk paper bags (1-5kg) ($0.10-0.30 per bag). First impression = price. Quality packaging + fresh mushrooms = premium customers = repeat buyers = higher prices.

Step 6: Store Properly for Shelf Life

Fresh storage: refrigerate at 5-10°C, 85-90% humidity, good air circulation. Shelf life: 5-7 days if stored correctly. Waste reduction: daily sales = minimal storage waste. Weekly farmers market = 30-40% waste (spoilage). Plan sales strategy: pre-orders, direct delivery, or accept some loss cost in margin calculations.

🚀 Real-World Project: Harvest & Sales Plan

Part 1: Harvest Readiness Checklist

Create daily harvest routine: inspect fruiting bags (ready to harvest?), prepare harvesting tools (clean knife, containers), cool/dry area setup (temperature, humidity), grading station (3 containers for A/B/C grades).

Part 2: Quality Grading Standards

Define Grade A, B, C specifications: cap size (cm), bruising allowed (none/minor/ok), appearance (perfect/good/sellable). Train 2-3 people on standards for consistency. Test: grade 50 mushrooms, verify all graders agree on at least 80% of grades.

Part 3: Packaging Specifications

Select packaging: retail (clamshells, kraft bags) vs. wholesale (bulk bags). Cost per unit: ___. Labels design: farm name, weight, harvest date, storage instructions, price. Calculate: packaging cost as % of revenue (target <5% of sale price).

Part 4: Storage & Cool Chain

Plan storage: refrigerator ($___) or cooler with ice ($10-20/week). Humidity maintenance: damp cloth, ventilation. Daily inventory: track freshness, removal of unsaleable product. Waste target: <10% spoilage (means good cold chain management).

Part 5: Yield Tracking per Bag

Record for each bag: inoculation date, Flush 1 harvest weight, grade breakdown (% A/B/C), Flush 2 weight & grades, Flush 3 weight & grades, total yield per bag. Average: ___ kg/bag across all flushes. Identify high vs. low performers (adjust technique for low performers).

Part 6: Revenue Calculation per Batch

100 bags × 0.9kg avg yield = 90kg total harvest. Grade breakdown: 40kg Grade A (×$9/kg = $360), 35kg Grade B (×$6/kg = $210), 15kg Grade C (×$3/kg = $45). Total revenue: $615. Costs (substrate, spawn, energy, labor): $300. Profit: $315 (51% margin). Per bag profit: $3.15.

Part 7: Sales Channel Strategy

Option A - Direct farmers market: $9-12/kg revenue, 10-15% waste. Option B - Restaurant wholesale: $5-7/kg, 5% waste. Option C - Supermarket: $6-8/kg, 8-10% waste. Calculate profitability for each, choose best fit for your operation.

Part 8: Growth Plan

Current: 100 bags/batch = $315 profit. Target year 2: 500 bags/batch = $1,575 profit (5x). Equipment & labor needed: second fruiting room? refrigeration upgrade? hire harvester? Timeline & investment: ___.
