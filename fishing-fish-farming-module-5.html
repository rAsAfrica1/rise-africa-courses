📋 6 Implementation Steps

Step 1: Set Up Sampling & Weighing System

Equipment: spring scale ($10-30), dip net, bucket. Monthly sampling: catch 50-100 fish randomly, weigh individually, calculate average weight. Track: month 1 weight (___g), month 2 (___g), month 3 (___g). Monthly growth rate = (current - previous month) / previous month × 100%.

Step 2: Track Survival Rate Meticulously

Initial stocking: 1,000 fingerlings. Count survivors periodically: month 1 (___ fish), month 2 (___ fish), month 3 (___ fish). Survival % = survivors / initial × 100%. Typical: 70-80% survival = healthy farm. <50% = major problem. Investigate losses: disease? predators? poor water?

Step 3: Project Harvest Yield

Formula: Expected yield = initial stocking × survival rate × average weight at harvest. Example: 1,000 × 75% × 0.6kg = 450kg total harvest. Expected revenue: 450kg × $3/kg = $1,350. Compared to costs ($750) = $600 profit. Update projections monthly as actual data emerges.

Step 4: Determine Optimal Harvest Timing

Watch growth curve: initially fast (exponential), then slows as fish approach market size. Market size: where buyers want it (typically 500-700g tilapia). When avg weight ≥ market size: ready to harvest. Earlier harvest = lower total yield but faster cash. Later = higher weight but risk of disease/death.

Step 5: Plan Harvest Logistics

Harvest date: coordinate with buyers (pre-sell if possible). Equipment: nets, buckets, transport containers, ice/cooling. Labour: 2-3 people for 1-day harvest. Handling: minimize stress, keep fish cool, avoid bruising. Transport: to market within 2-4 hours if selling fresh. Cost: ___. Revenue lost to poor handling: 5-10% (quality = price).

Step 6: Calculate Final Profitability & Plan Cycle 2

Final accounting: total revenue - total costs = profit. Profit margin: profit / revenue × 100%. Track: cycle 1 profit (___), identify cost savings for cycle 2. Reinvest profits: upgrade equipment? expand pond? better feed? improve survival rate? 3-year goal: double stocking density + reduce mortality = 3x profit.

🚀 Real-World Project: Harvest & Profitability Planning

Part 1: Sampling Schedule & Data Collection

Create monthly sampling calendar. For each month, record: date sampled, # fish sampled, average weight, growth rate %, survival %. Build growth tracking spreadsheet to visualize trajectory.

Part 2: Survival Rate Analysis

Track initial stocking vs. actual survivors at key points: week 2, week 4, month 1, month 2, month 3. Investigate any drops >5%: water quality? disease? overcrowding? predators? Document findings and corrective actions.

Part 3: Growth Projection Model

Build simple spreadsheet model: starting weight (10g fingerling), add monthly growth rate, project to market size (600g). Identify month when average reaches market size. Example: Month 1: 90g, Month 2: 170g, Month 3: 250g, ... Month 6: 600g (ready to harvest).

Part 4: Optimal Harvest Decision

Create harvest scenario analysis: if harvest month 5 (avg 500g) vs. month 6 (600g) vs. month 7 (650g)? Calculate: total harvest kg, total revenue, profit. Consider risks: prolonged culture = disease risk, water quality stress. Choose optimal timing based on data.

Part 5: Harvest Logistics Plan

Detail your harvest plan: target date, equipment needed (nets, buckets, scale, transport), labour hours & cost ($), buyer arrangement (pre-sold? market? restaurant?), post-harvest handling (immediate sale or iced storage?).

Part 6: Quality Control Strategy

Plan quality measures: fish handling to minimize stress/bruising, immediate cooling, transport time < 4 hours, grading if selling by size. Calculate quality loss risk (5-10% weight loss from spoilage). Impact on revenue: ___.

Part 7: Cycle 1 Final Profitability

Final accounting: Total revenue - Total costs = Profit. Breakdown: revenue (qty × price), costs (fingerlings, feed, equipment, labour, transport). Profit margin (profit / revenue × 100%). Identify top 3 cost savings for next cycle.

Part 8: 3-Year Expansion Plan

Cycle 1: baseline profit (___). Cycle 2: reinvest profits in improvements (larger pond? better feed? reduce mortality?). Cycle 3: expand to 2 ponds or increase stocking. Projected 3-year cumulative profit: ___. Identify funding needed vs. self-financing from profits.
