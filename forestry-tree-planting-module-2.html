📋 6 Implementation Steps

Step 1: Choose Your Nursery Model

Decision to make: Start backyard (100-500 m²) or go commercial (1+ hectares)? Consider your land availability, capital, and market demand. Most farmers start small and expand. A backyard nursery needs just $200-500 to start. For commercial scale, budget $1,000-3,000 for first year infrastructure.

Action: List available space, calculate startup budget, research local market demand for seedlings.

Step 2: Source Quality Seeds

Where to get seeds: Wild collection (free but risky), certified suppliers ($100-500/kg), international seed banks, or partnerships with existing nurseries. Priority species: Teak ($5-15/kg), Mahogany ($10-20/kg), Mango ($2-5/kg), Acacia ($1-3/kg).

Action: Identify 3-5 seed suppliers, order samples, test germination rates (should be 60%+ for quality seed).

Step 3: Prepare Germination Area

Setup needed: Seed beds or trays (1 m² = 500-1,000 seedlings), shade structure (50-75% shade cloth), water source, storage for seeds. Most critical: consistent moisture (not waterlogged) and temperature 20-30°C.

Action: Build or purchase germination trays, install shade structure, set up drip irrigation or watering system.

Step 4: Establish Seedling Growing Area

Space requirements: Once germinated (2-4 weeks), seedlings need 3-6 months of growth. Nursery bed area should be 2-3 times larger than germination area to prevent overcrowding. Plant spacing: 10-15 cm × 15-20 cm depending on species.

Action: Prepare growing beds with quality soil (mix topsoil + compost), install shade structures, set up nursery management records.

Step 5: Implement Health Management

Critical tasks: Watering (daily in hot season, every 2-3 days in cool season), fertilizing (NPK 10-10-10 starting week 3), pest/disease monitoring, hardening off (reduce shade 2-4 weeks before transplant). Common issues: damping-off (fungal disease - prevent with drains), leaf spots (spray with copper), insect damage (manual removal preferred).

Action: Create daily care schedule, set up pest monitoring system, keep health records for each seedling batch.

Step 6: Scale Operations & Profitability

Profit calculation: Seedling cost = ($500 setup + $50 seeds + $100 labor) ÷ 1,000 seedlings = $0.65/seedling. Selling price $1.50-$3.00/seedling = $0.85-$2.35 profit per seedling. 10,000 seedlings/year = $8,500-$23,500 gross profit after second year. Start with 1,000 seedlings (safe), scale to 5,000-10,000 as you gain confidence.

Action: Project sales for year 1 (conservative), year 2 (moderate growth), year 3+ (expansion). Save profits for equipment upgrades (shade houses, misting systems, better storage).

🚀 Real-World Project: Design Your Nursery Operation

Part 1: Nursery Plan

Sketch your nursery layout (1-2 pages). Include: total area, germination zone, growing zone, shade structures, water source, storage, office space. Specify nursery type (backyard/small/medium) and total capacity (seedlings/year).

Part 2: Seed Sourcing Strategy

Research 3 suppliers for your chosen species. Document: species name, cost/kg, min. order quantity, germination rates, delivery time. Calculate total seed budget for first 1,000 seedlings.

Part 3: Setup Cost Analysis

Create a spreadsheet: land preparation ($), shade cloth ($), germination trays ($), soil/compost ($), tools ($), seeds ($), labor ($). Total first-year setup cost. Budget for ongoing supplies (year 2, 3).

Part 4: Seedling Care Schedule

Create a 6-month calendar: Week 1-2 germination, Week 3-4 first fertilizer, Week 5-12 growth phase, Week 13-20 hardening off, Week 21-26 harvest. Include daily watering, weekly fertilizing, monthly pest checks.

Part 5: Profitability Projection

Calculate 3-year projections: Year 1 (assume 50% of capacity due to learning), Year 2 (80% capacity), Year 3 (100%+). Factor costs, revenue, profit margins. Break-even point: when cumulative profit exceeds setup costs?

Part 6: Market Research

Survey 5-10 local farmers/organizations: How many seedlings would they buy annually? What price per seedling? What species most needed? Document demand vs. your supply capacity.

Part 7: Risk Assessment

Identify 5 major risks (drought, disease, market collapse, theft, labor shortage). For each: likelihood (high/medium/low), impact (financial loss), mitigation strategy (how to prevent/reduce).

Part 8: Action Plan

Write your first 90-day nursery startup plan: Week 1-2 (site prep), Week 3-4 (build structures), Week 5-8 (source seeds, start germination), Week 9-12 (establish first seedling batch). Include timeline, costs, responsibilities, success metrics.
